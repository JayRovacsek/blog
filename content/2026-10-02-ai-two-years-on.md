**Note before we begin: this post is AI augmented but human crafted. The thoughts, structure and opinions are mine; an LLM helped get the words down. I expect I'll be flagging this on more posts, not fewer.**

It's been a touch over two years since I last put something in here. That feels about right for this repo; it has always been a fossil record of whatever arbitrary tech I'm chewing on rather than a steady stream.

The last time I wrote about AI specifically was [stable diffusion back in 2022](@/2022-08-31-stable-diffusion.md): a text prompt in, a cthulhu on sesame street out. Novel, a bit silly, and mostly about pictures.

The theme has well and truly moved. AI isn't something you visit anymore, it's threaded through the tools. The interesting conversation is no longer generation, it's agency: models that hold a tool belt, read your repo, run commands and sometimes commit on your behalf. My day job is security, so the shift from "look what it drew" to "what has it touched" is a large part of what occupies my brain now.

Coding is the clearest example of it. I've gone from avoiding autocomplete to leaning on agents for boilerplate, config and the boring 80%. When it works it feels like a genuine force multiplier; when it doesn't it's a very confident junior with commit access. The evaluation problem - did it actually do the thing, and can I prove it - is the same problem I wrestle with at work, just moving a lot faster.

The other theme I didn't predict is ownership. Running a model locally has gone from a novelty to something I actually want: no per-token bill, no data wandering off the network, no surprise deprecation. The gap between local and hosted is narrowing and, for a homelab person, the appeal is obvious.

No neat conclusion - just that the frame changed faster than I expected: from "what can it make", to "what can it do", and finally "who gets to run it".
